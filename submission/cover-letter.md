Dear Editor,

We submit for consideration in *[Journal]* the manuscript **"Deciduous-forward stocking standards at
the allowable cut: value and tail-risk trade-offs in the Prince George Timber Supply Area"**.

The paper asks whether the allowable annual cut is feasible on a real estate under even-flow
convergence, and what deciduous-forward stocking standards cost in value and tail risk at that cut.
It makes four contributions.

First, it corrects the production result. A state-based two-stage convergence meets or exceeds the
2025 cut of 6.917 million m3/yr in every period under all four standards: 19 periods sit at the AAC,
and the final even-flow reference period is 5.3 percent above the ceiling. The 200-year mean is 6.935
to 6.938 million m3/yr, 0.26 to 0.31 percent above the ceiling. An earlier fixed-flow search of this
estate did not find a feasible schedule; its 25 percent shortfall is not a land-base or yield limit.
The estate can sustain the cut, and the standards do not reduce it.

Second, it reports an enterprise value-and-tail surface at that level. At the BC Ministry of Forests
4 percent real rate, D26 raises net present value by 36.0 million CAD and reduces CVaR20 loss by
23.4 to 23.7 million CAD, a net CVaR gain of 59.4 to 59.7 million CAD. D55 costs 39.3 million CAD and
reduces CVaR20 loss by 38.8 million CAD under the calibrated retention scenario and 47.2 million CAD
under the observed record; it is approximately break-even. D84 costs 146.5 million CAD for a similar
reduction and is dominated by D55 on net CVaR. The observed record gives the larger absolute benefit
for D55 and D84; for D26 the two scenarios are nearly identical. D26 dominates the clearcut under both, and D55
dominates D84 on net CVaR under both.

Third, it reports the rate dependence. At 3 percent no standard pays; D26 is nearly neutral and D84
is worst. At 4 percent D26 is the value option and D55 the tail option. At 5 to 8 percent all three
standards improve net CVaR, and D55 is best at 8 percent. The ranking is conditional on the discount
rate; D84 is behind D55 on net CVaR at every rate, by 2.6 to 2.8 million CAD at 8 percent in both
fire-size scenarios.

Fourth, it states the fire-model boundary. The observed full 1,073-fire record and a calibrated
multi-year retention sample span the fire-size range we consider. The composition-aware fuel projection
reproduces 63.1 percent of the 2025 VRI fuel-type grid at t=0; the post-harvest S1 and O1B types
have no observational counterpart, and the D1 multiplier is the model's 0.2 surface-mortality floor,
not an estimate of D1 severity. The spatial fire-break question remains open.

The work continues the line of research on diversification under disturbance risk in European
forests (Fuchs et al., 2024, *Scientific Reports*), with two differences that matter for policy: the
disturbance is a process with spatial structure, and the decisions are made on an operating forest
under an allowable cut.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. The authors declare no
financial competing interests; one author develops the open-source estate model used in the work,
which is declared in the manuscript.
